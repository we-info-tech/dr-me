10 Money & Business
Lessons

A summary of the lessons Rishabh Dev shared in 10 Money & Business Lessons I Learned in the Past 10 Years ↗, kept here for quick reference.

1. Money as a Store of Value

Understanding money as a store of value is the key to financial success.

The money you have reflects the value you've generated for others. People exchange value for money and vice versa — when you provide value through products, services, or skills, you're compensated with money that represents that value. To grow wealth, focus on providing value to others: ask how you can serve people in a way that improves their lives or businesses.

2. Invest in Valuable Skills

Investing in skills is your best financial move in your 20s.

Skills are the foundation of financial independence. The best investment you can make early on isn't stocks or real estate, but yourself — specifically, developing skills that are in demand, like tech, data analysis, and marketing. Your earnings are often proportional to your skills, and while markets fluctuate, skills compound and generate long-term income.

3. Set Up a Business for Tax Efficiency

Setting up a business is a smart way to save on taxes and build wealth.

Taxes significantly impact your earnings when you're only earning as an individual. When you set up a business, many expenses become tax-deductible, from meals and travel to educational courses. This can be reinvested in the business or personal wealth, and is especially useful where personal tax rates are high but business tax rates are more manageable.

4. The Power of Long-Term Compounding

Long-term compounding is the way of building wealth over time on auto-pilot.

Compounding is the most powerful concept in wealth-building, yet it's often underestimated: returns on your investments grow over time, and those returns begin to generate their own returns. The longer you let investments compound, the more exponential the growth. This applies to financial investments as much as it does to skills and knowledge.

5. Focus on Growing Income Over Chasing Returns

Growing your income beats chasing higher returns.

Many people fixate on squeezing a few extra percentage points from their investments, but the real power lies in increasing income. Income growth has no cap, while returns from investments are limited by market dynamics — boosting income through side hustles, skill-building, or entrepreneurship lets you save and invest more.

6. Increasing Income is Easier Than Just Saving

Increasing income leads to greater wealth than purely saving.

Saving is essential, but there's a limit to how much you can save. Taking on a side job or freelance work can add significantly more to your income than cutting back ever could. A mindset shift toward earning more, rather than just spending less, can have an outsized impact on financial well-being.

7. The Difference Between Rich and Wealthy

True wealth often goes unseen.

Being rich and being wealthy are often mistaken as the same thing. Rich people show their wealth in tangible forms like cars and luxury goods, while truly wealthy people focus on growing their assets — their wealth speaks for itself in stability, investments, and long-term security. To build real wealth, prioritise investments and savings over lifestyle inflation.

8. Live Simply and Define 'Enough'

Defining enough helps you avoid chasing a moving goal.

Many people chase wealth without ever asking what "enough" means, and fall into an endless loop of wanting more. By defining your own "enough" — the point where you feel financially secure and fulfilled — you can avoid lifestyle inflation and focus on what truly matters. Wealth isn't about hoarding money; it's about freedom and security.

9. Compound Wealth Over Time

Time is your greatest asset: the case for long-term investing.

Small, consistent investments grow exponentially over time. If you start early, compounding can turn modest amounts into substantial wealth — avoid get-rich-quick schemes and high-risk investments, and instead focus on steady, long-term growth. A portfolio left to compound for decades often outperforms the most ambitious short-term investments.

10. True Happiness Comes from Freedom, Not Money

The real goal of financial success is finding freedom over wealth.

While money is essential, it's not the ultimate source of happiness. True happiness stems from freedom — the freedom to choose how you spend your time, where you live, and what you do. Money enables this freedom, but once you have "enough," the additional returns of wealth diminish in value. Real wealth is the ability to live life on your own terms.
