Seven Covers,
One Safety Net

Insurance is not where I expect to grow wealth. It is there to stop a major setback from forcing me to spend the wealth I am building.

I think of insurance as protection around my income, savings, family, and long-term investments. I currently hold six distinct covers and plan to add a super top-up later as my needs and required cover increase. Each layer has a different job; seven is my personal setup, not a number everyone needs to copy.

What each cover is there to do

Cover What I use it for How it can protect wealth Important limit
Cancer care A lump-sum benefit if an eligible cancer diagnosis meets the policy's stage definitions. Can help pay for treatment and give me money for time away from work or other household needs, instead of relying only on savings. Stage, payout, waiting period, survival period, and maximum benefit are controlled by the policy wording.
Personal accident Cover for defined temporary, permanent, or partial disability after an eligible accident. I have an adventure-activity add-on for the activities it specifies. Helps address the financial impact of an injury that could affect my ability to work, travel, or manage daily life. Benefits depend on the disability definition, assessment, exclusions, and any activity-specific terms.
Critical illness A benefit for a serious condition listed in the policy, if its diagnosis meets the policy definition. A serious illness can create costs and interrupt earnings even when it is not fatal. A defined benefit can provide financial room during recovery. The covered-condition list, severity criteria, survival period, and payout rules matter; this is not a claim about illness being statistically more likely than death.
Renter's cover Protection for insured household contents at my rented home against losses covered by the schedule and wording. Can reduce the cost of replacing belongings after a covered event, rather than using savings or owing money to replace them. My schedule shows contents cover and a terrorism extension. Jewellery, gold, and liability to a landlord need to be specifically insured; I do not assume they are covered unless listed.
Family floater health Shared health cover I use for eligible treatment for family members, including hospitalization for illnesses such as dengue or typhoid when the policy terms are met. Helps keep eligible medical bills from consuming savings set aside for other goals. It is a shared limit. Outpatient visits, small cuts, and every routine expense are not automatically covered; eligibility and limits depend on the policy.
Individual health A separate health-cover limit I intend to keep available for larger treatment needs, such as a covered knee replacement or heart surgery. Provides another pool of cover so one person's treatment does not rely only on the family's shared floater limit. Using the floater for smaller eligible claims is my own claim-management preference, not a restriction on what either policy covers. Bonus rules and claim effects vary by policy.
Super top-up (planned) An additional layer of health cover above a chosen deductible, which I plan to consider as my cover needs grow. Could increase protection against a very large eligible hospitalization bill without having to buy the entire added amount as base cover. I do not hold this yet. The deductible, claim aggregation, covered people, waiting periods, and exclusions must fit the underlying cover and my budget.

How my cancer-care benefit works

The cancer cover is a lump-sum benefit, not just reimbursement of individual treatment bills. If a claim qualifies, that flexibility matters to me: treatment can be costly, and a diagnosis may also affect how much I can work. I can use the payout to manage eligible treatment-related needs and the wider financial disruption, according to my circumstances.

Eligible diagnosis or progression Benefit described in the brochure How to read it
Early stage 50% of the sum insured, capped at Rs. 10 lakh. An early-stage claim has a lower payout and a specific rupee cap.
Major stage 100% of the sum insured. If the cover has already paid for early-stage cancer, a later eligible progression can trigger an additional benefit, subject to the policy maximum.
Advanced stage 150% of the sum insured. This is the maximum total benefit stated in the brochure; a prior early-stage payout is counted within the progression structure.

The brochure lists a 90-day initial waiting period (180 days for an early-stage claim) and a seven-day survival period. The policy's cancer-stage definitions, eligibility requirements, exclusions, and issued schedule determine whether a claim is payable. The published percentages describe this cover's structure, not a guarantee that every diagnosis will qualify.

Why I plan to add a super top-up

As I get older, the amount of health cover I may need can increase. A super top-up is one way I may add a higher layer for a large hospitalization bill while keeping an existing health policy as the first layer. It is not active cover for me yet, and the deductible and terms will matter as much as the headline sum insured.

Potential advantages Trade-offs and checks
Can add a larger layer of protection for high eligible medical costs, often at a lower premium than buying the entire increase as base cover. The deductible must be reached before the top-up pays. I need to know whether my base cover and savings can meet it.
A super top-up commonly counts eligible claims over a policy year toward the deductible, which can help when there are multiple claims. Aggregation rules vary. I need to confirm whose claims count, which expenses qualify, and whether the deductible is per person or shared.
Can sit above existing individual or family health cover as a catastrophic-cost layer. Different insurers or policy terms can mean separate claims processes, waiting periods, exclusions, co-payments, or limits. Premiums can also rise with age and renewal terms.
Lets me revisit how much protection I need as my responsibilities and medical-cost exposure change. It does not replace suitable base health cover, and a high sum insured is not useful if the deductible or conditions do not work for my situation.

A standard top-up and a super top-up can apply the deductible differently. A super top-up commonly aggregates eligible expenses across the policy period, but I will check the exact wording before choosing one.

Protection for the wealth I am building

My reason for having several covers is not that more policies automatically mean more safety. Each one is meant to address a different kind of financial shock: illness, disability, medical bills, or loss of household contents. If cover responds as intended, it can reduce the chance that I need to sell long-term investments, take on debt, or divert money from my family's goals to handle a sudden cost.

I still need to review premiums, overlaps, exclusions, deductibles, and the actual cover amounts. Insurance is a cost, and claims are subject to the contract. This is my personal approach to protecting wealth, not a recommendation that everyone should hold the same policies.
