The Intelligent Investor
— modern takeaways

Core philosophy

  • Focus on long-term wealth preservation, not short-term riches.
  • Be an intelligent investor by using rational thinking, patience, and discipline.
  • Avoid being driven by emotions, media hype, or crowd behavior.

Market volatility is normal

  • View the market like "Mr. Market" — an emotional partner offering daily prices.
  • Don't follow the crowd during greed (booms) or fear (crashes).
  • Use volatility to your advantage — buy low, sell high, or hold tight.

Margin of safety

  • Only invest in stocks when there's a buffer between the intrinsic value and market price.
  • The wider the margin, the safer the investment.
  • Today's equivalent: look for undervalued stocks or index funds, avoid overhyped IPOs or meme stocks.

Defensive vs. enterprising investor

Defensive investor (most of us):

  • Stick to index funds and ETFs (e.g., Nifty 50, S&P 500).
  • Focus on diversification and low-cost investing.
  • Minimize churn — don't check your portfolio daily.

Enterprising investor (requires time and skill):

  • Analyze individual stocks deeply — fundamentals, cash flow, management.
  • Focus on value investing, not speculation.
  • Today, includes screening via modern tools (like Screener.in, TIKR, etc.).

Speculation vs. investment

  • Investment: based on analysis, safety of principal, and expected returns.
  • Speculation: based on predictions, market trends, or noise.
  • Crypto, NFTs, and options trading fall under speculation (unless you're an expert with a strategy).

Inflation awareness

  • Graham talked about the risk of "money illusion" — true today more than ever.
  • Cash loses value over time due to inflation.
  • Invest in inflation-beating assets: equity funds, REITs, TIPS, gold, and real estate.

Stock picking: what to look for

  • Consistent earnings growth
  • Strong cash flows
  • Low debt-to-equity
  • High return on equity (ROE)
  • Avoid glamour stocks (trending for no reason)

Dividend investing

  • Dividends provide stability and income during downturns.
  • Today: focus on dividend growth stocks with solid fundamentals.

Behavioral discipline > stock-picking skill

  • Your temperament is more important than IQ.
  • Avoid panic-selling, overtrading, and FOMO buying.
  • Automate SIPs in index funds to remove emotion.

Risk management

  • Risk ≠ volatility; risk = losing money permanently.
  • Diversify: don't put all eggs in one basket (e.g., not 100% in tech stocks or crypto).
  • Know your risk appetite and adjust asset allocation (equity vs. debt) accordingly.

Timeless wisdom from Benjamin Graham

"The investor's chief problem — and even his worst enemy — is likely to be himself."

Still true today with FOMO, overtrading, and crypto hype.

"Price is what you pay. Value is what you get."

Don't confuse a high price with quality — look for undervalued opportunities.

"The essence of investment management is the management of risks, not the management of returns."

Focus on preserving capital, not chasing quick returns.

"In the short run, the market is a voting machine but in the long run, it is a weighing machine."

Popularity moves the market short term; fundamentals win in the long run.

"The intelligent investor is a realist who sells to optimists and buys from pessimists."

Be contrarian when others are emotional.

"Even the intelligent investor is likely to need considerable willpower to keep from following the crowd."

Discipline beats intelligence in investing.

My thoughts

  • Stick to fundamentals, not influencers.
  • Real wealth = long-term compounding, not flashy returns.
